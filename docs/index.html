Solar Syndicate

A spaceship management simulation set in our solar system. You are not the captain, and not a character at all — you are the guild, working an operations desk. You hire the crew, authorise the money, and set the policy the ship runs on. The people aboard are not you; they are your responsibility.

Play in your browser Source on GitHub No install, no account. Works offline.

Why it exists

Most space games put you in the pilot's seat. This one asks a different question: what is it like to be responsible for people you never meet, aboard a machine you cannot touch, across distances where even a message takes twenty minutes?

Time runs without you

One real hour is one game day. Close the tab and the crew keep working, sleeping, and eating; parts keep wearing out. Reopening replays what happened rather than pausing reality.

Honest physics

Real values, uprated 25–50% for near-future tech. ISS-derived metabolic rates, a 22 kWe reactor that dumps three times its output as waste heat, water loops that close to 97% — and don't, once worn.

You manage, you don't fly

Your instruments are hiring, the watch bill, spending, and orders sent across a light-delay. Whether the ship flies well depends on who you hired and what you asked of them.

What the game actually is

The same ship, eight game-days apart. On the left, freshly signed over to you. On the right, after being left with the engines drawing more power than the ship can make — no disaster, no death, just a machine following the consequences of a decision nobody came back to revisit.

Coming back

The rule the whole offline model is built around: nothing permanent happens without warning and a decision you could have made.

Emergencies open a window for your authorisation. Unanswered, they resolve on your standing orders and the ship's own interlocks — conservative by default, costly but survivable. The ship protects itself; it just charges you for it.

Crew ageing, ambitions and permadeath arrive in a later milestone. Today the ship can be wrecked; the crew can only be worn down.

The crew are people, not slots

Everyone has stats, skills, health and fatigue, and a metabolism that tracks what they are actually doing — four people asleep are a measurably smaller load than four people working.

Their quality reaches you only through numbers you already watch. Order a repair at ship midnight and your best engineer is asleep, so the captain picks it up at a third of her skill and takes twice as long. The fix is not a button; it is the watch bill.

Jobs hand over between watches and move at the pace of whoever holds the spanner — and slow down further if the air has gone bad.

Getting it

It runs in any modern browser with nothing installed. Installing it is optional and only changes how it launches — the game itself is identical, and works offline either way once loaded.

Any browser

Open the game. That's the whole step. State is saved locally in your browser, so the ship keeps running between visits.

iPhone / iPad

Open it in Safari, then Share → Add to Home Screen.

Why this differs: iOS only permits installing a web app from Safari's share sheet — Chrome and Firefox on iOS cannot do it, because they are all Safari underneath with that capability withheld. Installing is also a prerequisite for notifications on iOS 16.4+, which a game you check on twice a day will eventually want.

Android

Chrome usually offers an install prompt; otherwise menu → Add to Home screen / Install app.

Why this differs: Chrome supports the install prompt, so Android can offer installation rather than hiding it in a share menu.

Desktop

Chrome and Edge show an install icon in the address bar. Firefox and Safari on desktop have no equivalent — bookmark it instead; nothing is lost.

Why this differs: desktop Firefox removed its site-install feature, and Safari's is Mac-only and inconsistent, so a bookmark is the honest advice rather than instructions that won't work.

From source

git clone https://github.com/CoderCoop/SolarSyndicate
cd SolarSyndicate
pnpm install
pre-commit install    # secret scanning before commits
pnpm dev              # http://localhost:5173

Why this differs: contributors need the pre-commit hook, which players obviously don't. pnpm check runs the tests; pnpm verify drives the built app in a real browser.
